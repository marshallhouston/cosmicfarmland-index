# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Visitors who followed a link from marshall's writing, a GitHub repo, or a subdomain app and want to see what else lives here. Mostly developers and product people browsing on desktop or phone, in a curious low-commitment mood. Secondary: marshall himself, using the toolshed as a quick reference to his own skills, commands, and plugins.

## Product Purpose

The apex landing for cosmicfarmland.wtf. It routes visitors to the apps on subdomains and catalogs the skills, commands, and plugins they were built with. Success is a visitor clicking through to an app or a source link, and leaving with the sense that one person tends this whole garden.

## Positioning

One person's working farm of apps and tools, shown as a living catalog rather than a resume.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: click through to a live app card.
- Secondary CTA: view source on GitHub from a toolshed entry.
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "this person grows software like crops."
- Belief ladder: this is real and alive (live status dots, counts) -> the apps are interesting (blurbs, tags) -> the tooling behind them is open (toolshed with source links).
- Proof on hand: the live apps themselves and the generated catalog counts; no testimonials, none needed.

## Brand Personality

Hand-painted, homegrown, openly weird — the Grayton Beach design system. The
whole identity comes off one photograph of a hand-lettered welcome sign: a
stained board, marigold and forest-green letters, wet foliage, pine straw. It
answers to that town's slogan, **nice dogs, strange people**: friendly and
unguarded in how it talks, unsanded in how it looks. Voice is lowercase and
plainspoken ("grown by marshall", "nothing in this patch yet"), never corporate.

## Anti-references

Not a SaaS landing page: no pricing-tier grids, no hero metrics selling growth,
no testimonial walls. Not a cold sci-fi terminal aesthetic; the dark is stained
wood, not a hacker screen. Not a LinkedIn-style portfolio with headshots and
skill badges. Not beach-town kitsch either: no surfboards, no seashells, no
script-font "coastal" clip art — the reference is the sign, not the gift shop.

## Design Principles

- The farm metaphor is load-bearing: crops, soil, patches, the toolshed. Copy and iconography stay in-world.
- Everything shown is real: counts are generated from the actual plugin repo, statuses reflect deployed apps.
- Small site, full atmosphere: wood grain, foliage, mulch and film grain do the brand work so the content can stay plain.
- Route, don't retain: the page succeeds when the visitor leaves for an app or a repo.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA. Two themes — board (dark, default) and daylight (light) — so contrast is checked against both grounds. Respect prefers-reduced-motion for the entrance animations and the canopy sway. All interactive controls keyboard-operable with visible focus.
