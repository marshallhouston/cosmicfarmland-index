01 · The number

83.5 average, and 3.5 double bogeys a round

The scoring average is respectable. The double-bogey rate is what stands between you and a good 36 holes here, and almost none of it comes from the tee.

Across 108 holes you made 1 eagle, 3 birdies and 47 pars: par or better on 47% of holes. Then 21 holes went double or worse, 50 strokes over par between them. Convert half of those to bogeys and the average round is an 81.

The strokes-gained split says the same thing from the other direction. Against ShotPattern's 5-index benchmark you are +9.5 off the tee across six rounds and −31.6 with the short game and the putter. You are not losing this golf course with the driver. You are losing it with a wedge and a putter.

04 · The war on double bogeys

Twenty-one holes, and a very short list of causes

Every hole that went double or worse, tagged with what ShotPattern flagged on it. Every one of them carried at least one tag; twelve carried more than one stroke of blame.

Twelve of the twenty-one involved a second chip, a short-game shot that failed to reach the putting surface. That is the most common cause in your record here, ahead of three-putts (7) and penalties (6).

The tags also say where they are not coming from. Only four of these holes were short-sided, and only five bogeys all told came from inside 150 yards. Most of the damage is a chunked or bladed chip from ordinary rough.

05 · Penalties

Ten strokes, nine shots, 1.7 per round

Low by most standards, but eight of the nine turned into a bogey or worse, and the two from the tee cost two full shots each.

Five of nine came on approach shots rather than tee shots, and they follow one shape: a drive into the rough, then a long recovery that finds trouble instead of the fairway. Holes 8, 11, 16 and 17 account for six of them.

A tee ball that finds the fairway here plays to +0.48 on the hole; one that finds rough plays to +0.60, almost nothing. A tee ball in a penalty area plays to +2.00. You can play out of the rough at Meadow Hills. The penalty areas cost a shot and a half.

06 · Putting

35.3 putts a round, 19 three-putts in six rounds

The largest single leak in the record: −16.4 strokes gained across six rounds, −2.7 a round. Speed on the long ones, more than line.

Fifteen of the nineteen three-putts started from 20 feet or more. Your own rule, finish the first putt inside 10% of the starting distance, passes on 45% of the long ones (19 of 42), with a median leave right at 10%. The rounds where you clear it comfortably are the 77s.

From short range the record is fine: 85% from inside five feet. The damage is in the approach putt that sets up the short one.

07 · Approach, tee, and the short game

Everything changes at 175 yards

44% greens in regulation, 23% scrambling, 33% fairways, and a driver that is the best club in the bag.

Inside 175 yards you are a plus player: +5.3 strokes gained across 63 shots from 100 to 175. Beyond it you are −7.9 across 35 shots, and from 200+ you have not hit a single green in 21 attempts. Past 175 the course starts taking strokes back.

Off the tee: median drive 285 yards, 33% fairways, +9.5 strokes gained. The length is buying more than the accuracy is costing. The short game runs the other way at −15.2 across 76 shots, and from rough specifically, 15 of 43 failed to reach the green.

11 · The tee sheet

Club off every tee

The Shot Pattern course preview's expected strokes gained for each club from every tee, read against your own miss pattern. Driver is the play on ten of the thirteen par 4s and 5s.

The case for the driver is not that it is straight. Driver carries the best expected strokes gained on twelve of the thirteen, hole 5 the only exception. It is that the mid-irons are the weak spot: 7-iron 22% greens, 6-iron 9% greens, both around 70 feet of proximity, against 70 to 75% for the wedges. Driver median 285 with a 107-yard 90% width and 75% of the misses right, and it still grades out ahead, because the club it hands you next is a wedge.

So the shape of the round is driver to inside 130, then a wedge. A layup that leaves a 6-iron or 7-iron trades a good club for a bad one. With the ZXi7s 150 is a 9-iron, so a layup that leaves 150 is fine, which is exactly why 11 and 12 are the two holes worth clubbing down on.

12 · The read

What the data says to do on Saturday

01

Take the rough. Do not take the penalty area.

A drive in the rough costs you 0.12 of a shot versus the fairway here. A drive in a penalty area costs 1.5. With 33% fairways and 285-yard median distance the driver is still the play on 10 of the 13 par 4s and 5s, the exceptions being 5, 11 and 12. The discipline is on the second shot out of rough, which is where five of your nine penalties came from.

02

Wedge to the middle, always.

You are +5.3 from 100 to 175 and −3.4 from inside 100. The short approach is the worse shot, and short-siding yourself is what makes the chip that follows so expensive. Twelve of twenty-one doubles started as a missed green that needed two chips. On 6, 10 and 16 that means laying up to 110-120 and hitting a full 50°, never a long iron at the green.

03

Take the shorter iron on 17 and 15.

The two long par 3s have cost you +2.83 combined per round. In twelve tries you have hit three greens and made six 5s or worse, and the wreckage came from the long clubs: a triple on 17 with the hybrid in the water, a double with the 7-iron. The tee sheet says 7-iron on 15, and 8-iron on 17 when it plays 160-180 the way it did in Sep 2025. Anywhere on the putting surface is a win, and a bogey on 17 is a good score.

04

Spend the warm-up on 30-footers, not 6-footers.

85% from inside five feet is fine. The 45% pass rate on the 10% lag rule is the problem. Fifteen of nineteen three-putts started outside 20 feet, so first-putt speed is what to rehearse before the first tee.

05

Play the front nine to score and the back nine to survive.

Your front nine here averages +5.2 and your back nine +8.3. Sixteen of the twenty-one doubles came on holes 9 to 12 and 15 to 18. A bogey on 17 is a good score.

How this was built

  • Round, hole and shot records read straight out of ShotPattern's local store on your Mac (6 Meadow Hills rounds, 108 holes, 492 shots) and rebuilt shot by shot rather than read off the app's summary tiles. Two rounds (Sep 1 and Sep 21, 2025) had to be opened in the app first to pull their shot data down from the cloud.
  • Hole scores are highest shot number plus any penalty on the final shot, with duplicate shots de-duplicated by shot number. One duplicate tee shot turned up, on Sep 1, hole 14. Every round's reconstructed total matches the total ShotPattern itself reports.
  • Differentials are raw: (113 ÷ slope) × (score − course rating), using the rating and slope ShotPattern stores per round. No net-double-bogey cap, so they sit slightly above posted handicap differentials, but they are consistent across all six rounds, which is what matters for comparing them.
  • Strokes gained figures are ShotPattern's own, measured against the 5-index benchmark set in your profile. Distances are as the app recorded them; putt and short-game distances are shown in feet, full shots in yards.
  • ShotPattern's record on this course begins Sep 1, 2025. Anything you played at Meadow Hills before that is not here, and nothing on this page comes from anywhere else.
